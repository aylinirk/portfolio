Brief

The brief

The brief was not “build another networking app”. It was more specific than that.

  • Make contact capture fast.
  • Keep it low-profile in the middle of a conversation.
  • Don’t make users type under pressure.
  • Make the data usable after the event, not trapped in the app.

That meant the UX had to protect momentum. Every extra field, tap or decision was friction.

Problem

The problem

Conference networking is messy by default. People exchange cards, move on to the next conversation and trust their memory to do the rest. That usually doesn’t work.

Photos of cards end up in one place. Voice notes are somewhere else. Details get lost. CRM entry becomes a separate task. By the time the event is over, the best context is already fading.

Design principles

Four principles to design around

1

Capture first. Organise later.

The first job is to save the contact and the context before they disappear.

2

One clear next action.

In a rushed situation, the interface can’t ask users to think twice.

3

Use the fastest native behaviour.

Taking a photo and speaking a note are faster than filling in forms.

4

Reduce the work after the event.

The product only works if it saves time later, not just during capture.

Process

How it came together

0102030405
  1. 01

    Start with the real moment of use

    Users were not sitting at a desk. They were standing at booths, moving between talks or talking to the next person in line. Their attention was measured in seconds. The app couldn’t behave like a traditional form flow.

  2. 02

    Reduce the core flow to two actions

    The main action is scanning a business card. The camera opens straight away and OCR puts the details into structured fields. The second action is a voice note. One quick flow, one structured record, ready for export or CRM sync.

  3. 03

    Prove the workflow in low-fi

    Before high-fi, I built a working low-fi prototype with Figma Make, connected a Supabase database and deployed it on Vercel. We didn’t ask people whether they liked the screens. We checked whether the product worked in a realistic situation.

  4. 04

    Move to high-fi only after the behaviour worked

    Once the core interaction had proved useful, the goal was clarity, not decoration: a clearer hierarchy, less hesitation and two core actions that feel obvious and calm.

  5. 05

    Iterate from the first real friction point

    The first big friction point was organisation. Instead of tagging every contact during capture, I designed a session-based grouping model: you set the current conference once, and every new capture takes on that context.

Key UX decisions

The decisions that shaped the product

Make scanning the primary action

The app opens with the fastest, most useful action, not a dashboard.

Use OCR instead of manual entry

The contact is created before the next conversation starts.

Voice instead of typing for context

Speaking is faster, more natural and fits the rhythm of the moment. The app transcribes the note automatically.

Open to other systems

Clean handoff to HubSpot, Salesforce, Airtable or CSV. A contact that stays locked in the app is only half the job.

Validation

How it was tested

First, I compared how much useful context users captured during the event with and without the app. I looked at how detailed the notes were, which details people remembered and how useful the information was afterwards.

Second, I measured how much desk work was left after the event. For this, I timed the sorting, structuring and CRM handoff before and after.

The test group was small and early, so I see the results as a direction, not proof. But the signal was strong.

What users said
Reflection

When time and attention are limited, UX has to remove thinking before it adds features.

What I take away

ConCap confirmed a simple rule: in the moment, the product’s job is not to be clever. It’s to get out of the way.

The strongest decision was to reduce the experience to two core actions and test that behaviour early in a live prototype. That kept the work honest.

What comes next

The next step is not more capture. It’s better organisation and follow-up: session-based grouping by conference, audience or client segment, better filters and cleaner exports.

The key is to add that structure without slowing down the core capture flow. Speed is still the product.
