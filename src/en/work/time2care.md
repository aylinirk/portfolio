blocks:
  - label: Product
    heading: The product
    paras:
      - time2care is a mobile app that allows users to find, filter and book therapy and training appointments quickly and easily. All from their smartphone, without phone calls or long waiting times.
      - "The problem: booking therapy appointments still mostly happens by phone or email. That’s a real barrier, especially in the evenings and at weekends."
      - "The goal: an intuitive app that makes search, filtering and booking so simple and transparent that users find an appointment in just a few steps."
  - label: Research
    heading: Understanding the users
    paras:
      - "At the start I assumed that finding the right therapist was the biggest hurdle. After user interviews and an unmoderated remote usability study with five participants, it became clear: the booking process itself is also a problem. A lack of transparency and uncertainty about booking confirmation were the most common pain points."
insightQuote: Not just simplifying the search, but improving the entire journey through to confirmation.
insightAttr: Key insight from the research phase
prinLabel: Findings
prinHeading: 4 identified pain points
principles:
  - number: "01"
    title: High administrative effort
    body: Many users can only book by phone or email. This takes time and deters working people who want to book in the evenings or at weekends.
  - number: "02"
    title: No central solution
    body: Information about specialisations, qualifications and availability is hard to find or incomplete.
  - number: "03"
    title: Complex interface
    body: Not all users are good with technology. Older people in particular can quickly feel overwhelmed. The design needs to be intuitive and accessible.
  - number: "04"
    title: Uncertain booking confirmation
    body: Users are often unsure whether their appointment is really confirmed. Unclear confirmations lead to distrust and callbacks.

  - number: "01"
    title: Persona & user journey

  - number: "02"
    title: Lo-fi wireframes
    body: "Paper wireframes first, to test different approaches for the booking flow, then digital wireframes. Focus: a clear search with filtering, so Jonas reaches the right therapist without detours."
  - number: "03"
    title: Usability study
    body: An unmoderated remote study with 5 participants revealed where users get stuck in the flow. This was before the hi-fi design.
  - number: "04"
    title: Hi-fi prototype
    body: "The colour concept is oriented around calm and trust: a dark sage green as primary colour, light neutral surfaces, clear typography. Each screen reflects a concrete decision from research."

blocks2:
  - label: Validation
    heading: What the tests revealed
    paras:
      - "The three key findings from the usability study, and how the hi-fi design responds:"
    items:
      - title: Filters were unclear
        body: "Users overlooked the filter icon. Solution: filters as visible chips below the search bar, active filters highlighted."
      - title: Too many steps to book
        body: "Solution: direct access to available appointments from the detail view, without detours."
      - title: Missing confirmation page
        body: "Solution: a dedicated confirmation page with all booking details and a clear success message."
pull: Assumptions are not insights. *Testing* is.
reflection:
  - My initial assumption that search was the main problem was only half the truth. It took the interviews and usability tests to show that uncertainty about the booking confirmation carries just as much weight.
  - time2care was my first complete UX process, from research to hi-fi prototype. The course had a fixed process and little time. So I had to set priorities, and that helped me design more clearly.
nextSteps:
  - With more time I would deepen the accessibility work, especially for older users. I would also run a second usability test with the hi-fi prototype to check whether the revisions actually helped.
