Brief

The brief

The Spielgruppe Seon needed a website. Not just a page, but an online presence that parents could trust and find easily. The goal: give families the information they need to decide if the group is right for their child, and make it easy to get in touch.

There was no existing site, no brand guidelines and no template to start from. Just a need and a deadline.

Problem

The problem

Parents looking for a local playgroup rely a lot on first impressions. A missing or badly structured website looks disorganised. And for a group working with young children, trust is everything.

The challenge was to create a site that felt warm and welcoming without looking unprofessional. It also had to answer the most important questions (What happens there? When? For whom? How do I register?) without overwhelming new visitors.

Design principles

Design priorities

1

Answer the key questions immediately

Parents scan, they don’t read. Age range, schedule, location and registration had to be visible without scrolling.

2

Warm, not childish

The visual tone had to appeal to parents, not children. Trustworthy, friendly and clean, not busy or cartoon-like.

3

Low maintenance for the team

The group is run by volunteers. The site had to be simple enough for non-technical people to update without my help.

Process

How it came together

010203
  1. 01

    Understand the real audience

    The users were not the playgroup staff, but parents, most of them new to the area or new to the idea of a Spielgruppe. I started by mapping out what they would want to know and in what order.

  2. 02

    Define the information architecture

    I structured the content around the key questions parents have. What the group offers, who it is for, when and where it meets, and how to register. Simple, linear, no dead ends.

  3. 03

    Design and build

    I designed the layout in Figma first, then built the site. The visual language stayed clean and warm, with readable typography, soft colours and a clear hierarchy. That way, the content comes first.

Reflection

A small project with real stakes is still a real project.

What I take away

Spielgruppe Seon was my first end-to-end design and build project for a real client with real users. It showed me that even a simple brief needs real research. The users’ needs and the client’s needs are rarely the same.

It also confirmed that constraints (no budget, no brand, no existing content) are not obstacles. They are the brief.
